Questions to Ask Denver Real Estate Agents Before You Hire

Interviewing Denver real estate agents? Use these questions on pricing, marketing, offers, agreements and communication to compare agents and hire well.

By Ed Prather Real Estate·Published November 25, 2026·Updated November 25, 2026·6 min read

The essentials

The short answer.

The most useful questions to ask Denver real estate agents are the ones that reveal how they think: how they would price or evaluate a specific home, how they would market a listing or search for a buyer, how they compare offers, what the written agreement includes, and how they communicate when deadlines are tight. Ask every agent the same questions so you can compare answers fairly. The Colorado Division of Real Estate recommends speaking with more than one broker and choosing one who fits your needs. Colorado Division of Real Estate home-buying guide.

Keep these priorities in mind

  • Use the same interview questions with every Denver agent you consider.
  • Ask for reasoning tied to your property or search, not general claims.
  • Review the brokerage disclosure and agreement terms before you sign.

Hiring a real estate agent in Denver is a decision about judgment and process. Two agents can describe the same services in nearly identical words, yet approach pricing, negotiation and communication very differently. A structured interview helps you see those differences before you commit to a listing agreement or buyer agreement.

The questions below are grouped by topic, with separate lists for sellers and buyers. Print them, bring them to each conversation and take notes. If you are early in the process, our guides on selling a house in Denver and buying a home in Colorado provide useful background.

Questions about experience and licensing

Start with the basics, and verify what you hear. Search the agent on the Colorado Division of Real Estate’s license lookup to confirm an active license.

  • Which parts of Denver and the surrounding metro do you work in most often?
  • What types of properties do you handle regularly, such as detached homes, condos or townhomes?
  • Are you part of a team? If so, who would I work with at each stage?
  • Which brokerage relationship are you offering me, and can you walk me through the disclosure?

Colorado’s brokerage disclosure explains the differences between a seller’s agent, a buyer’s agent and a transaction-broker. Review the state’s disclosure form before your interviews so you can follow the answer.

Questions for Denver listing agents about pricing

Pricing is where an agent’s reasoning shows most clearly. Ask:

  • Which recent nearby sales would you use for my home, and why those properties?
  • How do you adjust for differences in condition, layout, lot and updates?
  • How would you respond if showings are steady but offers do not arrive?
  • What information about my home would change your recommendation?

A useful answer explains a range and the evidence behind it. Be wary of a precise figure offered before the agent has reviewed sales or seen the home. Our article on preparing a Denver house for sale covers the condition and document questions that often affect pricing.

Questions about marketing and showings

For sellers, ask how your home will be presented and who handles each step:

  • What does your listing preparation process include, and what would you recommend I fix or leave alone?
  • Who handles photography and the written description?
  • How are showings scheduled, and how will you share feedback?
  • How often will you update me, and in what format?

The team’s home marketing page and listing preparation page describe how Ed Prather Real Estate approaches these steps, which can serve as a reference point when comparing agents.

Questions for Denver buyer’s agents

Buyers should focus on search strategy, offers and deadlines:

  • How will you help me decide which homes are worth touring?
  • How do you evaluate a list price against recent sales?
  • What terms besides price might strengthen an offer, and what are the tradeoffs?
  • How do you approach inspection findings and negotiation?
  • Who tracks contract deadlines, and how will I be reminded?

Expect a buyer’s agent to raise the written agreement early. The National Association of REALTORS® explains that agents who use an MLS and work with buyers must sign a written agreement before touring a home, and the agreement must clearly state compensation and that fees are negotiable. NAR consumer guide to written buyer agreements.

Questions about offers and negotiation

Negotiation is more than price. Ask how the agent compares offers or builds one:

  • How do you compare offers with different financing, deadlines and contingencies?
  • How do you handle multiple offers?
  • What happens if an appraisal comes in below the contract price?
  • How do you communicate with the other side, and how quickly will I hear about responses?

Related guides on appraisal gaps and inspection objections explain the Colorado contract steps that often come up in these conversations.

Questions about agreements and compensation

Ask to see the agreement you would sign and read it before the meeting ends. Useful questions include:

  • How long does the agreement last, and how can it end early?
  • What services are included?
  • How is compensation determined, and when is it paid?
  • Are there any fees beyond the stated compensation?

Brokerage compensation is not set by law and is fully negotiable, as NAR’s consumer materials note. Take your time with the terms, and ask for clarification in writing if anything is unclear.

How to score your Denver agent interviews

After each conversation, rate the agent on a simple scale for four areas: clarity of reasoning, relevance to your property or search, communication plan and transparency about the agreement. Add notes on anything that surprised you.

Pricing or search strategySpecific nearby sales and the reasoning behind adjustments

Frequently asked questions

How many Denver real estate agents should I interview?+

Two or three is usually enough to see meaningful differences. The Colorado Division of Real Estate recommends contacting more than one broker and choosing one who fits your needs and knows the areas you are considering.

What is the most important question to ask a listing agent?+

Ask which recent nearby sales they would use to price your home and why. The answer shows whether the agent’s recommendation is grounded in specific evidence and how they think about condition, layout and timing.

Can I negotiate a Denver real estate agent’s commission?+

Yes. Broker fees and commissions are not set by law and are fully negotiable. Ask the agent to explain the compensation terms in the listing agreement or buyer agreement, and make sure you understand them before you sign.

Should I hire the agent who suggests the highest list price?+

Not on that basis alone. A higher suggested price is only useful if it is supported by nearby sales and a realistic plan. Compare the reasoning behind each recommendation and ask how the agent would respond if the market reaction differs from expectations.

Put your research to work

Use one question list for every interview.

Consistent questions make it easier to compare agents fairly. Keep your notes on a single page so you can see differences in reasoning, process and communication at a glance.

If you plan to sell and buy, ask each agent how they would coordinate both timelines, and whether the same person or team would handle each side.

Bring your timeline

Share your intended timing and any fixed dates, such as a lease ending or a job start. Those details change the strategy an agent will recommend.
