How to Find the Best Realtor Near Me: A Denver-Area Checklist

Looking for the best realtor near me in Denver or Lakewood? Use this checklist to verify licenses, read reviews well and compare local knowledge.

By Ed Prather Real Estate·Published November 25, 2026·Updated November 25, 2026·7 min read

The essentials

The short answer.

The best realtor near me is rarely just the closest one. In the Denver area, start your search with a short list, verify each broker’s license with the state, read reviews for patterns, and then compare how each agent explains your specific market, your timeline and the written agreement you would sign. The Colorado Division of Real Estate notes that many buyers work with brokers referred by prior clients and recommends contacting more than one broker before choosing. Colorado Division of Real Estate home-buying guide.

Keep these priorities in mind

  • Build a short list from referrals, reviews and agents who work regularly in your target areas.
  • Check every name on the state license lookup.
  • Choose based on clear answers about pricing, process and communication, not proximity alone.

A “near me” search returns a map full of options. Physical distance matters less than it seems: a Colorado broker’s license covers the whole state, and most of the work happens through showings, documents and phone calls. What matters is whether an agent understands the homes and areas you are considering across Denver, Lakewood and the surrounding Front Range, and whether they communicate in a way that works for you.

This checklist shows how to move from a long list of nearby realtors to one you trust. It applies whether you are buying your first home, selling, or relocating to the Denver metro from out of state. If you are new to the process, the step-by-step guide to buying a home in Colorado is a good companion.

Define “near me” by where you want to live

Before contacting anyone, list the cities or parts of town you are seriously considering. For one household that might be Lakewood and Wheat Ridge; for another, central Denver and Englewood. The best realtor near you is one who regularly works in those places, can describe property types and HOA patterns there, and can arrange showings efficiently.

The team’s community guides cover Denver, Lakewood and the surrounding Front Range, with local resources for maps, zoning and transportation. Use them to narrow your search area, then ask each agent how they would approach those specific places.

Verify the license before anything else

The term realtor is often used for any real estate agent, but the legal requirement in Colorado is a state real estate license. Search each name on the Division of Real Estate’s license lookup and confirm the status is active. Pending, inactive or expired licensees may not perform actions that require a license.

Also note the brokerage the agent works under. If the agent is part of a team, ask which broker would sign your agreement and who else would support you.

Read the best realtor reviews for patterns

Star ratings are a starting signal, not a decision. Read several reviews on more than one platform and look for repeated themes: responsiveness, preparation, honesty about tradeoffs and calm handling of problems. Pay attention to reviews that describe a situation similar to yours.

  • Do reviewers mention specific help, such as pricing advice, inspection negotiations or deadline tracking?
  • Do reviews reference the agent you would actually work with?
  • Are recent reviews consistent with older ones?

Ed Prather Real Estate holds a 5.0 rating across more than 1,000 reviews on its Google Business Profile. You can read additional client experiences on the client stories page. Use what you read to shape interview questions rather than as the final answer.

Compare Denver real estate agents on substance

Once you have two or three names, schedule short conversations and ask each the same questions. For buyers, ask how they would evaluate a list price against recent sales, how they approach inspections and what they would want you to have ready before making an offer. For sellers, ask which nearby sales they would use for pricing and what preparation they would prioritize.

Notice whether the answers are specific to your situation or generic. A strong agent asks you questions in return: your timing, budget, must-haves and what would make you walk away from a home. Our article on questions to ask Denver real estate agents provides a fuller interview list.

Understand the agreement before you tour

If you are buying, expect to discuss a written agreement early. The National Association of REALTORS® explains that agents who use an MLS and work with buyers must sign a written agreement with the buyer before touring a home, and that the agreement must disclose compensation in an objective way and state that fees are fully negotiable and not set by law. NAR’s summary for homebuyers.

In Colorado, you will also receive a brokerage disclosure describing whether the broker will work as your agent or as a transaction-broker. Read both documents and ask questions until the services, duration and compensation are clear. The state’s home-buying guide notes that you may wish to seek legal advice from an attorney before entering into a buyer agreement.

Get financing questions moving at the same time

Choosing an agent and talking with a lender can happen in parallel. A preapproval or prequalification letter helps you and your agent focus on homes that fit your plans. The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau explains that both letters are statements from a lender about how much it may be willing to lend, and that neither is a guaranteed loan offer. CFPB on prequalification and preapproval letters.

A good agent will ask about your financing early because it affects which homes make sense to tour and how an offer is written. For first purchases, the first-time homebuyer page outlines what to prepare.

Signs you have found the right fit

After your conversations, the right choice is usually the agent whose explanations you understood and trusted. Look for these signs:

  1. They explained their reasoning instead of just giving a number.
  2. They were clear about what they do and do not handle, and when you need another professional.
  3. They described how they will keep you informed and who to contact if they are unavailable.
  4. They walked through the written agreement without rushing you.
  5. They treated your questions as part of the process, not an obstacle.

Frequently asked questions

Is the closest realtor always the best realtor near me?+

Not necessarily. A Colorado license covers the whole state, and most of an agent’s value comes from local market knowledge, preparation and communication. Prioritize agents who regularly work in the cities or parts of town you are considering, then compare how clearly they explain pricing, process and the written agreement.

How do I check if a Colorado realtor is licensed?+

Use the Colorado Division of Real Estate’s online license lookup and search by the broker’s name or license number. Confirm the license is active. Pending, inactive or expired licensees may not perform actions that require a license.

What is the difference between a realtor and a real estate agent?+

In everyday use the terms are often interchangeable. REALTOR® is a trademarked term for members of the National Association of REALTORS®. The legal requirement to help someone buy or sell in Colorado is an active state real estate license, so verify the license regardless of the title used.

Do I need an agent to buy a home in Colorado?+

No. The Colorado Division of Real Estate says you are not required to be represented by a broker to buy or sell, but because the process is complex, it recommends hiring a licensed real estate broker.

Put your research to work

Turn your search results into a shortlist of three.

Pick up to three agents who work in your target areas, verify their licenses and read their reviews. Then schedule short conversations using the same questions so you can compare answers fairly.

Keep notes on how each agent explained their reasoning and how clearly they described the written agreement. Those notes will make the decision straightforward.

Start with your plan, not paperwork

Share your timing, the areas you are considering and whether you are buying, selling or both. Leave financial documents for a secure step later in the process.
