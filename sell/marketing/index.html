The marketing plan, step by step

What the listing marketing plan includes.

Marketing starts before the listing goes live. The plan connects pricing, presentation and exposure so buyers see your home at its best, and you hear how buyers respond.

1. Pricing and strategy

A comparative market analysis of recent sales and competing listings, a recommended listing price and a marketing approach for your property and timeline.

2. Presentation

Staging recommendations, preparing the home for professional photography and guidance on minor repairs and decluttering.

3. Exposure

Online listings, digital promotion, social media and agent networks to reach buyers searching in your area.

4. Showings and feedback

Coordinated showings and open houses, with buyer feedback shared so you can discuss adjustments.

5. Offers

Each offer is reviewed for price, financing, contingencies and timing, then negotiated toward your goals.

Start with the property, not a template

Discuss your goals, timing, condition, and practical constraints before choosing a marketing approach. Ask for a written plan that explains the audience, preparation priorities, and launch sequence.

Make the presentation deliberate

Walk through the photography plan, room preparation, video opportunities, and the features buyers should understand. Agree on what will be prepared, who will do it, and the budget before work begins.

Connect exposure to useful feedback

Ask how your home will be presented online and how inquiries and showing feedback will reach you. Decide in advance when to review activity and which signals would prompt a change.

Evaluate the whole offer

Compare more than the headline price. Ask your agent to walk through financing, timing, concessions, contingencies, and the costs that affect your net proceeds.

Put your research to work

Connect your home’s presentation to a clear buyer decision.

A marketing plan should make it easier for a prospective buyer to understand the property. Begin with a verified description of the layout, condition, inclusions and features that matter to everyday use. Keep unsupported claims about investment returns, future development or guaranteed performance out of the message.

Discuss the launch materials, intended channels and showing arrangements with your agent before publication. Ask what will be prepared, what you need to approve and which details must be confirmed before they appear publicly. Marketing decisions should follow the listing agreement and the plan for your particular home.

Prepare the story and the supporting records

Create a feature sheet that distinguishes original features, documented improvements and items needing further investigation. Pair renovation descriptions with available dates, permits or receipts rather than relying on a broad “fully updated” label. Photograph-ready rooms and a complete information folder serve different purposes, so plan both.

Use feedback to choose the next action

Agree on how you will review showing comments and relevant competing properties. Separate repeated concerns from isolated preferences, and identify whether the response should involve access, presentation, additional information or pricing discussion. A marketing adjustment should answer evidence from the actual listing period, not a promised formula for a fast sale.
