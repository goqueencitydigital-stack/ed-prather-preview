01

Home Pricing and Comparable Market Analysis

Pricing decides who sees your home and how it compares once buyers start touring. We prepare a comparative market analysis for your property before you list and recommend a competitive listing price.

  • Recent comparable sales near your address
  • Current competing listings
  • Your home’s condition, improvements, layout and features
  • Local buyer demand and how it may affect timing
  • Estimated selling costs and proceeds; see seller closing costs in Colorado
02

Preparing Your Home for the Market

Not every home needs major work before listing. We help you decide which repairs, decluttering and presentation changes are worthwhile for your property, local competition and budget.

  • Staging recommendations
  • Preparing the home for professional photography
  • Prioritizing minor repairs and decluttering
  • Read the Denver home preparation guide
03

Marketing Your Home to Potential Buyers

Your marketing plan is tailored to the home’s features, the buyers most likely to want it and the listings it competes with.

  • Professional property presentation for photos, online listings and showings
  • Online listings, digital promotion, social media and agent networks
  • Showing coordination and open houses
  • Buyer feedback shared after showings so you can discuss adjustments
  • See how we market Denver and Lakewood homes
04

Reviewing Offers and Negotiating Your Sale

A strong offer is more than the price. We review each offer with you and negotiate with your priorities in mind.

  • Price, financing type and appraisal terms
  • Inspection and other contingencies
  • Closing date and possession timing
  • Concession requests and how to respond; see appraisal gaps in Colorado
05

Coordinating Your Home Sale and Next Purchase

If you are buying your next home too, the same team plans both sides. We help align closing dates and can use tools such as leaseback provisions so you have a place to live through the move.

From inspection and appraisal requirements to documents and closing, we coordinate with the buyer’s side, lenders and escrow. Learn how to sell and buy at the same time.

Good questions.
Clear answers.

Where should I start?+

Start with a conversation about your property, your goals and your timeline. A consultation helps identify the pricing questions, preparation decisions and next steps specific to your move.

Will you arrange repairs or cleaning for me?+

We offer guidance on preparation priorities. You decide which repairs, cleaning and decluttering tasks to undertake and arrange the work with professionals you select.

Is a home valuation an instant estimate?+

No. The request starts a property-specific conversation with the team. It does not generate an automated value or guarantee a sale price.

Can we plan a sale and a purchase together?+

Yes. Tell us about both sides of your move so we can discuss your goals and sequencing. Financing, contract terms and availability still need individual review.

Are you listing agents or a cash home buyer?+

Our selling service is listing representation. The team helps you price, prepare, market and negotiate the sale of your home to buyers on the market. Ask during your consultation about the options that fit your timeline.

Which areas do your listing agents cover?+

Denver, Lakewood and surrounding Front Range communities. Start with the Denver or Lakewood guide for local context.

What does a home valuation include?+

A review of recent comparable sales, current competing listings, your home’s condition and features and local buyer demand, followed by a conversation about a realistic pricing range. Request yours here.

Put your research to work

A stronger sale starts with clear decisions, not a generic checklist.

Before choosing a list date, decide what the sale needs to accomplish. Your priorities may include releasing equity for another purchase, reducing upkeep, relocating or simplifying an inherited-property situation. Write down the timing constraints and the decisions you can keep flexible so your preparation plan reflects your circumstances.

Separate the work into information, presentation and negotiation. Information includes the property records and questions a buyer may raise. Presentation covers how the home will be prepared and shown. Negotiation concerns the full terms of an offer, including dates, financing, inspection conditions and possession.

What if the home needs repairs?

Describe the known issue and gather any reports, service records or written estimates already available. Discuss whether further evaluation would help you decide between completing work, adjusting the presentation or considering offers in the home’s current condition. Do not assume a cosmetic update resolves a condition question or that selling without repairs removes disclosure considerations.

When should you list?

Choose a launch window by working backward from readiness and your next housing plan. Photography, access, documentation and decision-maker availability all need attention. Request a dated comparison of relevant competing and recently sold properties before making a timing or pricing decision; a broad seasonal rule cannot answer those questions for every home.

What should you compare when offers arrive?

Use a written comparison of proposed proceeds, contingencies, deadlines and possession arrangements. Keep estimated proceeds separate from confirmed closing figures. Colorado’s sales-contract guidance describes these transaction provisions and recommends appropriate professional advice on their consequences.
