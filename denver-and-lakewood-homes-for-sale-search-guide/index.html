Denver and Lakewood Homes for Sale: A Smarter Search Guide

Search Denver and Lakewood homes for sale with smarter filters, careful listing review, zoning and HOA checks, and a tour plan that helps you act fast.

By Ed Prather Real Estate·Published November 25, 2026·Updated November 25, 2026·7 min read

The essentials

The short answer.

To search Denver and Lakewood homes for sale effectively, start with your financing, set filters around the features that truly matter, review each listing for what it leaves out, verify property-specific details such as HOA documents and zoning, and plan tours in batches. Set up alerts so new listings reach you quickly, and keep a simple comparison sheet. The choice of where to live is yours; fair housing law protects your right to choose a home without discrimination or steering. HUD overview of the Fair Housing Act.

Keep these priorities in mind

  • Get financing questions answered first so your filters reflect a realistic budget.
  • Read listings for missing information and verify details with documents.
  • Use alerts and a comparison sheet so you can act quickly and consistently.

Searching for homes for sale in Denver and Lakewood can be exciting and overwhelming at the same time. Listings change daily, photos can flatter or hide details, and the same budget can mean different things depending on property type, location and HOA. A clear search process saves time and helps you recognize the right home when it appears.

This guide covers how to set up your search, read listings carefully, compare Denver and Lakewood properties fairly and plan tours. You can browse current listings through the team’s Colorado MLS home search, or share your preferences on the home search preferences page so the team can help.

Start with financing, then set your filters

Your budget shapes every other filter, so talk with a lender early. The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau explains that prequalification and preapproval letters are both statements from a lender about how much it may be willing to lend, and that neither is a guaranteed loan offer. CFPB on prequalification and preapproval.

Then think about the full monthly cost, not just the price. Taxes, insurance, HOA dues and maintenance all matter. The true cost of owning calculator lets you test scenarios with your own assumptions before you set a price filter.

Keep must-have filters few: price range, property type, minimum bedrooms and any essential feature such as main-floor living or a garage. Too many filters can hide homes that would work well.

Compare Denver homes for sale and Lakewood homes for sale fairly

Lakewood sits directly west of Denver, and many buyers search both. Compare them on property-level facts rather than general impressions. U.S. Census Bureau QuickFacts estimates from the 2020 to 2024 American Community Survey show a median value of owner-occupied homes of $574,400 in Lakewood and $616,000 in Denver. U.S. Census Bureau QuickFacts. These five-year estimates describe whole cities and lag the current market; they are not a sale price or a valuation for any home.

Within each city, property types vary widely, from detached homes to townhomes and condos. For each listing, compare the same items: price, property type, size and layout, condition, HOA dues and rules, lot characteristics and your actual commute. The Denver community guide and Lakewood community guide link to city maps, zoning and transportation resources.

Read every listing for what it does not say

Listings are marketing. They highlight strengths and may leave out details that matter to you. As you review homes for sale, note questions such as:

  • What is the age and condition of major systems, such as the roof, furnace and water heater?
  • Is there an HOA, and what do the dues cover?
  • Are there photos of every room, or are some spaces missing?
  • How long has the home been on the market compared with similar homes nearby?
  • Has the price changed, and is there a disclosed reason?

Your agent can help answer these questions or request information from the listing side. For older homes, our guide to buying an older home in Denver covers records and inspection questions.

Verify property-specific details

Some questions can only be answered for a specific address. Use official sources where possible. Lakewood publishes city maps and Denver provides zoning and permit information. If you are thinking about an addition, an accessory dwelling or a home business, ask the city about the specific parcel rather than relying on a general rule.

For condos and townhomes, request HOA documents early and review budgets, reserves, rules and insurance. Our HOA document guide explains what to look for. For every home, plan for an inspection and consider radon testing; the radon guide explains how testing fits into a Colorado purchase.

Search by property, not by who lives nearby

The federal Fair Housing Act prohibits discrimination in housing based on race, color, national origin, religion, sex, familial status or disability. HUD’s Fair Housing Act overview. In practice, that means agents should describe properties and point you to objective resources, not tell you which areas are right for certain kinds of people.

Focus your search on what you can measure: the home’s features, its condition, ownership costs, commute times and access to the places you go regularly. If schools, parks or transit matter to you, look at official sources directly and form your own view. A good agent will help you find the information without steering your choice.

Use alerts and tour in batches

New listings can move quickly, so set up alerts that match your core filters. You can create searches through the team’s Colorado MLS home search or tell the team what you are looking for on the home search preferences page.

When several homes look promising, tour them in a batch. Seeing homes back to back makes comparison easier. Use the tour planning page to share the properties you want to see and your preferred times. If you are buying with an agent, expect to sign a written buyer agreement before touring; NAR explains that it applies to both in-person and live virtual tours. NAR consumer guide to written buyer agreements.

Keep a comparison sheet

After each tour, write down the same details for every home so your memory doesn’t do the comparing for you:

  1. Address, list price and property type.
  2. Estimated monthly cost, including HOA dues if any.
  3. Condition notes and questions for an inspector.
  4. Layout and storage observations.
  5. Commute test results for your actual routine.
  6. Your overall rating and the main reason for it.

The compare homes tool can help you organize these notes. When a home stands out, your comparison sheet makes it easier to move forward with confidence.

Frequently asked questions

Where can I search Denver and Lakewood homes for sale?+

You can browse current listings through the team’s Colorado MLS home search, or share your preferences on the home search preferences page so the Ed Prather team can follow up. Set alerts around a few core filters so new listings reach you quickly.

Are Lakewood homes less expensive than Denver homes?+

It depends on the specific property. Census Bureau estimates for 2020 to 2024 show a lower median value of owner-occupied homes in Lakewood than in Denver, but those city-wide figures lag the market and are not a price for any home. Compare individual listings on property type, condition, size and ownership costs.

How do I know if a home’s price is fair?+

Compare it with recent sales of similar nearby homes, adjusting for condition, size and features. Your agent can prepare that comparison. An appraisal may also be part of a financed purchase; our appraisal gap guide explains what can happen if it comes in below the contract price.

Can an agent tell me which neighborhood is best for me?+

An agent can share objective information about properties, costs and commute options, and point you to official resources. Fair housing law means the choice of where to live is yours, and agents should not steer buyers toward or away from areas based on protected characteristics.

Put your research to work

Make your home search a repeatable routine.

Set a few core filters, review new listings on a schedule and record the same details for each home. A consistent process keeps you from missing good options or second-guessing decisions.

When you are ready to tour, group homes by area and schedule them together. Bring your comparison sheet and fill it in before you leave each property.
