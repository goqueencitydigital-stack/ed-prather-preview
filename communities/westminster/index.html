Find your place in Westminster.

Compare Westminster addresses through a practical combination of trail access and transportation. Begin with your regular destinations, then choose the outdoor routes and property features that would make an ordinary week work better for you.

Photo credit: Nyttend

Explore Colorado · A practical guide

Use Westminster’s regional trail network as one starting point for comparing locations.City of Westminster trail guide Review city maps for context, then confirm parcel-specific land use, plans and permits directly with appropriate city staff before making decisions.

01

Start with Westminster’s trail network

Westminster’s outdoor network is a useful way to begin comparing locations: it includes concrete, gravel, natural and multi-surface routes, as well as five regional trails.City of Westminster trail guide The five regional trails are Big Dry Creek, Farmers’ High Line Canal, Little Dry Creek, Rocky Mountain Greenway and the U.S. 36 Bikeway.City of Westminster trail guide Consider which route and surface you would actually use, rather than treating trail access as a simple yes-or-no feature.

The city describes the Big Dry Creek Trail as the crown jewel of its off-road trail system.City of Westminster trail guide Farmers’ High Line Canal Trail winds from near Standley Lake east toward Northglenn’s EB Raines Park and beyond into Thornton.City of Westminster trail guide For a home search, use those documented destinations to plan a visit, then check the specific home-to-trail connection, crossing points and route conditions yourself.

02

Check routes, not commute claims

The U.S. 36 Bikeway extends from 80th Avenue at U.S. 36 to 96th Street at U.S. 36, midway between Broomfield and Louisville.City of Westminster trail guide The Little Dry Creek Trail connects Westminster to the Denver regional trail system.City of Westminster trail guide These are concrete route details to compare against your own plans, but they do not establish travel times or prove that a particular address has convenient access.

Before choosing a property, consider tracing the trips you expect to make, including where a route begins and ends and whether it works for your intended travel mode. Westminster’s trail page links to an interactive map and a printable trail map.City of Westminster trail guide Use those resources as a starting point, then verify the current route and access details directly; avoid relying on generic commute estimates.

03

Use city maps for community context

Westminster’s Maps page provides a Comprehensive Land Use Plan Map approved in March 2023.Maps | Westminster, CO It also offers Westminster in the Works, an interactive city map that includes citywide improvement projects, active construction projects and a snow plow tracker during snowstorms.Maps | Westminster, CO These tools can help you identify questions about a location, but they are not a substitute for confirming a specific property’s status.

The city also provides a Westminster HOA Map showing reported HOA boundaries, with contact and detail information for HOAs.Maps | Westminster, CO If an HOA may affect your decision, consider using the map to identify a possible contact, then confirm the association and current documents for the property independently. For any map, note what it does and does not establish before treating it as a basis for a purchase decision.

04

Understand the planning documents

Westminster defines its Comprehensive Plan as the city’s primary planning document: a long-term guide that shapes decisions about new development and redevelopment and provides a framework for Planned Unit Development zoning.Planning | Westminster, CO The city describes a Planned Unit Development, or PUD, as a zoning district allowing flexibility for a unified approach to mixed-use design, with an approved Preliminary Development Plan required for PUD-zoned properties.Planning | Westminster, CO

A Preliminary Development Plan, or PDP, sets out a property’s basic development framework, including allowed uses, standards, lot arrangement and access points.Planning | Westminster, CO An Official Development Plan, or ODP, is a site-specific plan for property within an approved PDP and can include site, grading, landscape, lighting and architectural plans.Planning | Westminster, CO Treat these definitions as a guide to what to ask about, not evidence of a parcel’s rules.

05

Verify property details before you commit

Do not infer a home’s zoning, permitted use or development constraints from a citywide map or general description. Westminster says its Planning Division reviews proposed development and coordinates project review, while the city’s map page identifies a Comprehensive Land Use Plan Map rather than a parcel-specific determination.Planning | Westminster, CO Maps | Westminster, CO For a property under consideration, ask Planning which records and approvals apply to that address.

As a practical due-diligence step, consider checking the property’s recorded plans and any permit history relevant to changes you may be evaluating. Westminster’s Planning Division handles Special Use Permits and Conditional Use Permits, and reviews sign permits; the city directs people seeking other permits to its Permits and Licenses page.Planning | Westminster, CO Confirm the correct department and requirements for your specific question rather than assuming a general planning overview settles it.

06

Know how development review works

Westminster identifies pre-application review as the mandatory first step for most development applications, including PDPs, PDP amendments, ODPs and ODP amendments.Planning | Westminster, CO The city describes subsequent steps as project submittal, interdepartmental and inter-agency reviews, public hearings when applicable, checkprint review and document recordation.Planning | Westminster, CO If nearby development is part of your decision, ask what stage a proposal has reached and whether a hearing is required.

The city says the first round of project review takes four weeks, with second and later rounds taking three weeks each.Planning | Westminster, CO Those are review durations described by the city, not a guarantee of an overall project timeline or approval.Planning | Westminster, CO For an active proposal, consider confirming the latest status and next steps with Planning, especially before relying on a presumed project outcome.

07

Make a purposeful Westminster scouting visit

Plan a visit around questions you can answer on the ground. The city identifies Big Dry Creek, Farmers’ High Line Canal, Little Dry Creek, Rocky Mountain Greenway and the U.S. 36 Bikeway as regional trails.City of Westminster trail guide Choose a route relevant to a property you are considering, and check its surface, access and connection to your intended destination. The city’s interactive and printable trail maps can help you prepare.City of Westminster trail guide

After exploring, consider reviewing the city’s maps for the location: the land use plan, Westminster in the Works and the HOA map each provide different kinds of context.Maps | Westminster, CO Write down any questions about a nearby project, a proposed use or property-specific records, then direct them to the relevant city office. A deliberate visit is more useful than assuming that a trail, map or plan answers every ownership question.

08

Turn research into buying or selling next steps

For a buyer, consider organizing questions into three groups: the routes and destinations that matter to you, the city-map context you want to understand, and the property-specific records you need to confirm. Westminster’s Planning Division coordinates development review, and its contact number is 303-658-2092.Planning | Westminster, CO The Parks, Recreation & Libraries Administrative Office can be reached at 303-658-2192 for trail questions.City of Westminster trail guide

For a seller, consider gathering accurate property and improvement information and identifying questions that a buyer may want to verify with the city. Westminster says project-meeting notifications are typically mailed to addresses and property owners within 1,000 feet of a project site, and public-hearing notifications are typically mailed to addresses and property owners within 1,000 feet of a development-related hearing site.Planning | Westminster, CO Use city records and direct confirmation rather than making claims about a proposal’s outcome.

Before your next move

Questions & answers.

How extensive is Westminster’s trail system?+

Westminster has a network of multi-use trails with concrete, gravel, natural and multi-surface routes.City of Westminster trail guide It identifies five regional trails: Big Dry Creek, Farmers’ High Line Canal, Little Dry Creek, Rocky Mountain Greenway and the U.S. 36 Bikeway.City of Westminster trail guide Check a specific route before relying on it for regular travel.

Which Westminster maps can help me research an area?+

The city offers a Comprehensive Land Use Plan Map approved in March 2023, an interactive Westminster in the Works map covering improvement projects, active construction and snow plow tracking during snowstorms, and a map of reported HOA boundaries with contacts and details.Maps | Westminster, CO Use these for context, then verify property-specific details directly.

Can a city map tell me what I can build on a property?+

Not by itself. Westminster’s Maps page lists a Comprehensive Land Use Plan Map, while its Planning Division reviews development and administers planning documents such as PDPs and ODPs.Maps | Westminster, CO Planning | Westminster, CO Ask Planning which approvals and standards apply to the specific address rather than inferring parcel rules from a citywide map.

What is the difference between a PDP and an ODP?+

Westminster describes a Preliminary Development Plan as the basic framework for development, including allowed uses and development standards.Planning | Westminster, CO An Official Development Plan is site-specific and applies to property within an approved PDP; it can include site, grading, landscape, lighting and architectural plans.Planning | Westminster, CO Confirm which documents apply to a property with the city.

Who should I contact with a planning question?+

Westminster lists the Planning Division’s phone number as 303-658-2092.Planning | Westminster, CO For trail questions, the Parks, Recreation & Libraries Administrative Office is listed at 303-658-2192.City of Westminster trail guide When you call, have the property address or specific route question ready so you can ask for the relevant information.
