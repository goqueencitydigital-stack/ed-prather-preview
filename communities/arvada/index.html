Find your place in Arvada.

Start by deciding how much of your routine you want centered on Olde Town, then compare trail access and property requirements. This guide offers distinct local anchors without treating every Arvada address as the same lifestyle.

Explore Colorado · A practical guide

Compare candidate addresses against the places and routines that matter to you. Use city tools to check parks, construction, and zoning; visit at times that reflect your normal schedule; and keep confirmed facts separate from questions that still need answers before buying or selling.

Updated November 6, 2026

01

Start with Olde Town and the wider city

For a first orientation, explore Olde Town Arvada, a historic district on the National Register of Historic Places with residential living, shopping and dining, and one of Arvada’s three RTD G Line stops. The City describes the G Line as connecting riders with the wider metro region and Denver International Airport. Treat that as a transit option to investigate, not a promise about travel time or convenience from a particular home. City of Arvada: Olde Town Arvada

Use Olde Town as one comparison point, not a stand-in for every part of Arvada. Before choosing a search area, list the places and routines that matter to you, then compare how a specific address relates to them. A useful starting list might include rail access, shops, dining, parks, or a particular trail. Confirm routes and distances yourself, and revisit the locations at times that match your normal schedule.

02

Compare parks by the routine you want

Use the City’s park list or map to find parks you would actually visit and check their listed amenities and addresses. Then assess the route from a candidate property yourself; citywide park-access figures do not establish the distance or route from an individual home. City of Arvada: Park Inventory and Amenities

Match amenities to your own use rather than relying on a park’s name or size. The inventory lists a splash pad at Ralston Central Park, disc golf at Memorial Park, and four pickleball courts at Clear Creek Valley Park. These examples can help you build a short list for a visit. Check the city inventory for the amenities and address of each candidate, then test the walk or drive from homes you are considering. City of Arvada: Park Inventory and Amenities

03

Read Olde Town as a planned district

Olde Town has an adopted Strategic Reinvestment Plan intended to preserve the district’s history and character while supporting a vibrant downtown. City Council adopted it in October 2023, following a planning effort that began in June 2022 and included community engagement. The plan sets out a shared 20-year vision and guiding principles described as Vibrant, Distinct, Connected, and Resilient. City of Arvada: Olde Town Arvada

The City’s Olde Town page includes street-access and construction updates. City of Arvada: Olde Town Arvada Check the latest notices before visiting, then observe the streets, crossings and nearby businesses for yourself. Test the route to each property separately rather than assuming a district-wide plan establishes current access to a particular address.

04

Verify routes and construction at the address

The City’s Public Improvement Projects page provides a construction-project map for work in roads, rights-of-way, and pedestrian areas. Users can search by address or intersection, zoom in, and locate projects by type; the page cautions that the map does not display project status updates. Use it as a discovery tool, then follow any linked project page for details before drawing conclusions about a home’s access. City of Arvada: Public Improvement Projects

Not every road project in Arvada is a City project: the City identifies portions of West 64th Avenue, Highway 72, and Highway 93 as state highways under CDOT’s responsibility for approval and inspection. For a home you are considering, check the project map and confirm who manages relevant work. Separately test your likely routes at the times you expect to travel; do not rely on generalized commute estimates or a single visit. City of Arvada: Public Improvement Projects

05

Use zoning tools for the exact property

Arvada’s Land Development Code has 26 zone districts that define permitted land uses and development standards, including lot size and building placement. City rules also address topics such as landscaping, building design, signs, and accessory structures. Because these rules depend on the property, do not infer a home’s zoning from a nearby parcel, a neighborhood label, or a citywide overview. City of Arvada: Zoning

For an initial check, the City directs users to its Property and Address Search and Zoning District Interactive Map. For official verification, request a zoning verification from Planning and Zoning or call the Planning Counter at 720-898-7435. The City says an official letter can address zoning, conformity with the Land Development Code, and whether zoning violations exist. Use the address you are evaluating, not an approximate location. City of Arvada: Zoning

06

Pair zoning review with property records

A zoning check is only one part of reviewing a property. The City notes that requests for additional documents, including certificates of occupancy and building permits, may require an official Public Records Request when extensive research is needed. If a renovation, addition, or past use matters to your decision, identify the records you want and ask the City how to request them. City of Arvada: Zoning

For your own due-diligence checklist, consider reviewing available records alongside an inspection and any documents supplied for the sale. Write down questions about visible changes, intended use, and work that may need follow-up; then seek the appropriate records or professional advice. Keep separate notes for confirmed facts and unanswered questions. This makes it easier to compare properties consistently without treating a map result or a seller’s description as a substitute for address-specific verification.

07

Make one visit answer several questions

Plan a scouting visit around a specific address. Walk the immediate area, then visit a nearby park from the City inventory and, if relevant, explore Olde Town or check a G Line stop. The City’s park page provides addresses and listed amenities, while its Olde Town page describes the district and rail connection. These resources help you choose destinations; your own visit helps you assess whether the routine fits. City of Arvada: Park Inventory and Amenities City of Arvada: Olde Town Arvada

Before an offer, organize the next checks: confirm the property’s zoning, decide whether you need an official verification letter, look for relevant construction projects, and list records or property questions that remain open. If you are selling, gather accurate property information and note questions a buyer may reasonably want to verify. Keep each decision tied to the particular property and your priorities.

Questions & answers.

What is a useful first place to explore in Arvada?+

Olde Town is a practical first stop if you want to experience a historic district with shopping, dining, residential living, and a G Line station. The City identifies it as one of Arvada’s three G Line stops. Use a visit to assess the area for yourself, and verify transit details and routes for the address you are considering. City of Arvada: Olde Town Arvada

How can I check which parks are near a property?+

Use the City’s Park Inventory and Amenities page to search its park list or interactive map, then check the listed address and amenities for places near your candidate home. Confirm the route and distance from the property yourself. City of Arvada: Park Inventory and Amenities

How do I verify a property’s zoning?+

Start with Arvada’s Property and Address Search or Zoning District Interactive Map, using the exact property address. For an official zoning verification, email Planning and Zoning or call the Planning Counter at 720-898-7435. The City says an official letter can cover zoning, Land Development Code conformity, and whether zoning violations exist. City of Arvada: Zoning

Can I use the City’s construction map to confirm project status?+

The City’s map can help locate active construction projects by address, intersection, or project type, but the City says it does not display project status updates. Check any linked project page for more information. Also note that some state highways through Arvada are managed by CDOT, rather than the City, for highway construction and maintenance. City of Arvada: Public Improvement Projects

Where can I ask about building permits or certificates of occupancy?+

Arvada’s zoning page notes that additional documents, including building permits and certificates of occupancy, may require an official Public Records Request if extensive research is needed. If those records matter to your purchase, identify the documents you seek and follow the City’s records-request process rather than relying only on a zoning-map result. City of Arvada: Zoning
