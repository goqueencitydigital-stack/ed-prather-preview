Find your place in Centennial.

For a Centennial search, the useful questions are often highly local: which recreation district serves the address, which routes fit your routine and what responsibilities come with the property? This guide helps organize that comparison.

Explore Colorado · A practical guide

Compare prospective homes using an address-specific checklist: identify recreation-district responsibility, review zoning and development records, and personally check routes and amenities before deciding.

Updated November 6, 2026

01

Start with Centennial’s local framework

The City says many Centennial parks, trails and open spaces are managed by park and recreation districts rather than the City. City of Centennial recreation districts When comparing homes, identify who manages the nearby recreation options and who maintains a particular facility.

Centennial Center Park is beside the Centennial Civic Center and includes lawns, an amphitheater, a playground, a seasonal splash pad, accessible walking paths and gathering spaces. Centennial Center Park Treat these as amenities to investigate, not as a measure of a property’s value or a guarantee that every home has convenient access.

02

Recreation districts are not interchangeable

The City says South Suburban Parks and Recreation District serves residents in central and western Centennial; its local highlights include Goodson Recreation Center near South University Boulevard and Arapahoe Road, and Family Sports Center on Peoria Street. City of Centennial recreation districts If these facilities matter to your household, confirm which services, access rules and fees apply to the specific address and facility.

The City describes Trails Park and Recreation District as generally serving residents east of Parker Road and north of Arapahoe Road, and lists Trails Recreation Center and a neighboring skate park among its Centennial features. City of Centennial recreation districts The City also says Arapahoe County Recreation District manages Dove Valley Regional Park with passive recreation amenities, and Smoky Hill Metro District maintains neighborhood public areas, parks, a pool and clubhouse. City of Centennial recreation districts Check boundaries directly rather than assuming service from a broad description.

03

Test the everyday amenity routine

Centennial Center Park lists paved and unpaved walking trails, picnic tables, restrooms, shade areas, a playground and reservable shelters among its features. Centennial Center Park Consider which features you would actually use, then visit at a time that resembles your expected routine. The City lists seasonal park hours and water-play hours, so check its current information before planning around the splash pad. Centennial Center Park

The City describes the splash pad as operating from Memorial Day through Labor Day, with water-play hours from 10 a.m. to 8 p.m. Centennial Center Park The City lists the amphitheater, plaza and several shelters as reservable areas. Centennial Center Park For any prospective home, make your own short list of everyday destinations and verify their location, operating details and access independently.

04

Verify routes instead of relying on estimates

For transportation due diligence, map the actual trip from a property to the places you expect to visit regularly, then test the route at the times that matter to you. Check more than one route and note where you would walk, drive, cycle or connect with other transportation. This is a suggested verification process, not a claim about travel times, traffic conditions or the availability of a particular service.

Before visiting, check the City’s current park-access information; construction details and restrictions can change. Then separately investigate routes serving any home you are considering.

05

Look up zoning for the exact address

Centennial’s interactive zoning map lets users select a zone district, adjust the legend, zoom, reset map orientation and search for an address. Centennial Zoning Map Use the address search as an initial research step, then record the displayed district and consult the City’s zoning descriptions or Property Search resource for more detail. Centennial Zoning Map The map is a lookup tool, not a substitute for confirming how rules apply to a particular parcel.

The Community Development page identifies zoning administration, site plans, plats, building permits and inspections among the department’s responsibilities, and lists a Planning and Zoning Hotline at 303-754-3308. Community Development Before making plans for an addition, accessory structure, business use or redevelopment, write down your specific question and ask the City to confirm how its rules apply to the property.

06

Check development records and permits

Centennial’s Community Development page links to Active Development Permits and Active Development Plans maps for finding active permits and plans. Community Development Search the property area and nearby locations, then confirm what each record represents and whether its status or scope has changed.

The City says Community Development manages development review, including building permits and inspections, plats, site plans and zoning administration, and identifies the Land Development Code as the City’s primary set of development and zoning regulations. Community Development For a specific property, request or review records relevant to your intended use and ask the City what steps apply; do not assume that a map entry guarantees approval or predicts a project’s outcome.

07

Make property due diligence address-specific

Build a property checklist around the address, not general impressions of Centennial. Suggested checks include confirming the parcel’s zoning, reviewing available permit and plan records, identifying the recreation district, and asking who maintains nearby parks or trails. The City says park development and maintenance are supported by district mill levies and that it contributes county share-back and Conservation Trust Funds to improvements. City of Centennial recreation districts Verify any property-specific financial or service question with the appropriate source.

The City provides online options to apply for land-use projects, upload plans, check project status, pay fees and schedule or view inspections. Community Development These tools can help you locate municipal process information, but they do not replace reviewing records and conditions relevant to the particular home.

08

Scout, compare and organize next steps

For a concrete visit, begin at Centennial Center Park, walk the paths and public spaces, and note which features matter to you. The park is at 13050 E. Peakview Avenue, and the City provides an interactive park map. Centennial Center Park Confirm access details before going, then check nearby destinations from the prospective property address.

After the visit, compare homes using the same checklist: address-specific zoning and records, recreation-district responsibility, routes you have personally checked, and amenities you have actually evaluated. Buyers can organize follow-up questions for the City and property representatives; sellers can gather relevant property and permit information before preparing materials. Centennial lists centennialplanning@centennialco.gov and the Planning and Zoning Hotline at 303-754-3308 for planning questions. Community Development

Questions & answers.

Who manages parks and recreation facilities in Centennial?+

Management varies by location. The City says many parks, trails and open spaces are run by recreation districts rather than the City, including South Suburban, Trails Park and Recreation, Arapahoe County Recreation and Smoky Hill Metro Districts. City of Centennial recreation districts Identify the district for the specific address, then verify which facilities and services it manages.

How can I check zoning for a Centennial property?+

Search the address in the City’s interactive zoning map, then consult the City’s zoning descriptions or Property Search resource. Centennial Zoning Map Treat this as an initial check, not a parcel-specific ruling. For questions about a proposed use or project, contact Community Development or the Planning and Zoning Hotline at 303-754-3308. Community Development

What amenities does Centennial Center Park have?+

The City lists lawns, an amphitheater, a playground, a seasonal splash pad, gathering spaces, gardens and accessible walking paths at Centennial Center Park. Centennial Center Park The park also has picnic tables, restrooms, shade areas and reservable shelters. Centennial Center Park The City describes the splash pad as seasonal, operating from Memorial Day through Labor Day; check the City’s page for current visit details. Centennial Center Park

Where can I look for active permits or development plans?+

Centennial’s Community Development page links to Active Development Permits and Active Development Plans maps. Community Development Search the property area and nearby locations, then confirm the record’s current status and details with the City. The department manages development review that includes permits, inspections, plats, site plans and zoning administration. Community Development

What should I verify before relying on a route near Centennial Center Park?+

Before traveling, check the current access information on the Centennial Center Park page, then test routes from any home you are considering.
