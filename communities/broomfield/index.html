Find your place in Broomfield.

Use Broomfield’s trail and transit connections as starting points, then compare how an individual address fits your workday and recreation plans. The best shortlist is built around usable routes, documented ownership costs and the property itself.

Photo credit: TimTay

Explore Colorado · A practical guide

Broomfield’s trail resources describe the developing Broomfield Trail, regional paths, and the U.S. 36 Bikeway. Broomfield Trails Map This guide pairs those documented connections with the city’s parcel-level planning research map and a practical scouting checklist, so buyers and sellers can verify property-specific details and assess everyday fit rather than rely on assumptions. 01

A trail network with distinct roles

Broomfield’s trail system is more than one continuous route: the city describes the Broomfield Trail as a future route of almost 20 miles across the community, with completed sections passing through Interlocken, Lilac Park, Wildgrass, and Anthem. The Lake Link Trail is a mostly soft-surface route of about 11 miles, connecting Lac Amora Open Space on the west with McKay Lake on the east. Broomfield Trails Map

Other routes offer different experiences. The Big Dry Creek Trail extends from Standley Lake along Broomfield’s south and west edges, while Rock Creek and Coal Creek trails pass north and west of the city and follow creeks through farms and open space. For a property search, treat these as options to investigate on the ground, not as proof that a particular home is beside or connected to a trail. Broomfield Trails Map

02

Choose a route to test your routine

The U.S. 36 Bikeway is a multi-use concrete path running through Broomfield alongside the highway; the city describes it as part of a trail connection from downtown Denver to downtown Boulder using other trail connections. That makes it a useful route to inspect if biking is part of your routine, but it does not establish the best route from any specific address. Broomfield Trails Map

Before choosing a home, test the routes that matter to you at the times you would actually use them. Check the current path, crossings, access points, and any alerts in a mapping tool; Broomfield shares official trail data with Colorado Trail Explorer, which offers interactive maps, real-time alerts, and offline map downloads. For driving or other transportation, independently verify the route and timing rather than relying on a general estimate. Broomfield Trails Map

03

Map destinations, then confirm access

Broomfield identifies neighborhood, school, commercial-area, public-facility, and open-space connections as purposes of its trail system. The Southwest Community Loop Trail, in particular, connects original neighborhoods near Broomfield Town Square toward the city’s southwest edge and newer neighborhoods to the north; the city says future sections are intended to add connections to commercial areas and city services. Broomfield Trails Map

Use those named connections to create a personal checklist: which destinations do you want within a convenient trip, and which would you reach on foot, by bike, or by car? Then verify the actual route from each candidate property. The trail map is available at the Broomfield Library, Broomfield Community Center, Paul Derda Recreation Center, and Community Assistance Center, or can be requested by email. Broomfield Trails Map

04

Look up the parcel, not just the area

Broomfield’s planning research map lets users search a property address and view parcel information, including subdivision, property size, and zoning. The city directs people to this map to check a property’s zoning. Start with the exact address for any home you are seriously considering, and save the map results for follow-up questions rather than applying a nearby property’s designation to it. Zoning | City and County of Broomfield

The same planning research map can show whether a parcel is within an area plan or urban renewal area, along with other overlay areas. Broomfield also provides a Sub-Area and Neighborhood Plans Map and an Urban Renewal Areas Plan Map. Use these resources as prompts for more research about the specific parcel; the presence of a map layer alone should not be treated as a conclusion about a project or future change. Area Plans and Urban Renewal Plans | City and County of Broomfield

05

Understand what a zoning label means

The city distinguishes standard zoning from legacy county zoning and Planned Unit Development designations. A zoning label containing A, W, B, or J identifies a legacy zone district; a PUD label indicates a Planned Unit Development. Broomfield directs users to the relevant legacy county code for legacy districts, the Municipal Code for standard districts, and the Planning Division for questions about PUD parcels. Zoning | City and County of Broomfield

For a purchase, confirm the parcel’s exact designation and ask the Planning Division how the applicable code relates to your intended use or planned work. Broomfield notes that some uses permitted by zoning may still require a separate license, and that licensing is not handled by the Planning Division. This is a reason to verify both zoning and any required licensing before purchase, lease, or occupancy, not to assume either is automatic. Zoning | City and County of Broomfield

06

Compare daily fit with a checklist

Compare homes by how well each supports your own routine, not by assuming that one named destination or trail serves every address equally. Broomfield’s published trail descriptions identify connections through Interlocken, Lilac Park, Wildgrass, Anthem, Lac Amora, McKay Lake, and other named places; confirm which access point is practical from each property you are evaluating. Broomfield Trails Map

For each candidate, make a short list of the destinations and services you expect to use, then verify their location, hours, route, and accessibility directly. The city notes that many trails are ADA accessible, while some segments do not meet ADA guidelines for slope and trail width; consult its ADA Trail information before relying on a segment for a mobility device. Broomfield Trails Map

07

Make the scouting visit concrete

Plan a visit around two or three documented trail settings rather than trying to cover the entire network. For example, inspect a Lake Link section near Lac Amora, then compare it with a Big Dry Creek access point at Metzger Farm or Willow Run Open Space; the city identifies these as trail connections. Add a stop on the U.S. 36 Bikeway if that type of multi-use route matters to you. Broomfield Trails Map

At each stop, note the surface, access point, crossings, signs, and how the route feels at the time of your visit. Then drive or otherwise travel from the prospective property to the places you expect to use, checking current navigation information rather than assuming proximity from a map label. If you want to review the full system, use the city’s printed map or COTREX resources before planning the outing. Broomfield Trails Map

08

Turn research into clear next steps

For a buyer, keep a property file with the exact-address planning-map results, zoning questions, area-plan or overlay information, route checks, and observations from the scouting visit. Contact Broomfield Planning if the property has a PUD designation or if the applicable zoning code is unclear; the city lists planning@broomfield.org for zoning questions. Verify any licensing question through the city’s licensing resources. Zoning | City and County of Broomfield

For a seller, consider assembling the same address-specific information and pointing interested parties to the city’s official maps, without making unsupported claims about permitted uses or trail access. For either side, turn unresolved questions into a written list for the appropriate city contact, then confirm answers against the exact parcel and intended use. This keeps the decision process grounded in verifiable details instead of neighborhood-wide assumptions. Zoning | City and County of Broomfield

Questions & answers.

How can I check the zoning for a Broomfield property?+

Search the exact property address in Broomfield’s planning research map; the city says the parcel result includes zoning, subdivision, and property size. Check whether the label is a legacy district or PUD, since Broomfield directs users to different code resources for those designations. Contact the Planning Division if the designation or applicable code is unclear. Zoning | City and County of Broomfield

How do I find out whether a parcel is in an area plan?+

Use Broomfield’s planning research map and search the property address. The map can show overlay areas, and the city also provides Sub-Area and Neighborhood Plans and Urban Renewal Areas maps. Treat a map result as a starting point for parcel-specific questions; do not assume it establishes what can be built or changed. Area Plans and Urban Renewal Plans | City and County of Broomfield

Which Broomfield trails have the clearest documented route descriptions?+

The city describes the Lake Link Trail as a mostly soft-surface route of about 11 miles between Lac Amora Open Space and McKay Lake. The U.S. 36 Bikeway is a multi-use concrete path alongside the highway, and Big Dry Creek Trail connects with Broomfield at locations including Metzger Farm and Willow Run open spaces. Confirm the access point for your address. Broomfield Trails Map

Are Broomfield trails accessible for mobility devices?+

Many Broomfield trails are ADA accessible, but the city notes that some segments fall outside ADA guidelines for slope and trail width. Check the city’s ADA Trail information for the particular segment before relying on it for a mobility device. The general trail description alone does not establish that every part of a route is accessible. Broomfield Trails Map

Does zoning approval mean a use can begin?+

Not necessarily. Broomfield notes that some uses permitted by zoning may still require a license, which has a separate review process and is not regulated by the Planning Division. Before purchase, lease, or occupancy, confirm the parcel’s zoning and check the city’s licensing resources for any license relevant to the intended use. Zoning | City and County of Broomfield

Where can I get a Broomfield trail map?+

The city says complimentary printed maps are available at the Broomfield Library, Broomfield Community Center, Paul Derda Recreation Center, and Community Assistance Center. You can also request a mailed copy by emailing openspace@broomfield.org with your name and mailing address. The city is developing a new map for print in 2026. Broomfield Trails Map
