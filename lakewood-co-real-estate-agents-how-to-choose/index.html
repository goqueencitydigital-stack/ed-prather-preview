How to Choose a Real Estate Agent in Lakewood, CO

Interviewing agents in Lakewood, CO? Learn how to verify a license, test local pricing knowledge, understand brokerage roles and ask the right questions.

By Ed Prather Real Estate·Published November 25, 2026·Updated November 25, 2026·8 min read

The essentials

The short answer.

Choosing a real estate agent in Lakewood comes down to a few checks you can make before signing anything: confirm the broker’s Colorado license, ask how they would price or search using nearby Lakewood sales rather than metro averages, understand which brokerage relationship they are offering, and agree on how communication will work. The Colorado Division of Real Estate recommends that buyers contact more than one broker and choose someone who fits their needs and knows the areas they are considering. Colorado Division of Real Estate home-buying guide.

Keep these priorities in mind

  • Verify the license on the state’s lookup before relying on any title or credential.
  • Ask for a Lakewood-specific explanation of pricing or search strategy, not a metro-wide average.
  • Read the brokerage disclosure so you know whether the broker is an agent or a transaction-broker for you.

Lakewood sits directly west of Denver, so many people who search for a Lakewood real estate agent are also weighing homes in Denver, Golden, Wheat Ridge or Arvada. That makes the choice of agent less about who is closest and more about who can explain the specific streets, property types and contract questions in front of you. A useful agent helps you make decisions with documents and comparable sales, not slogans.

This guide walks through how to compare real estate agents in Lakewood, CO, whether you are selling a house, buying one or doing both. It focuses on checks you can verify yourself, the Colorado brokerage relationships you will see in writing, and the questions that reveal how an agent actually works. For a broader overview of the city itself, start with the Lakewood community guide. If you would like to talk with a Lakewood-based team, meet our Lakewood CO real estate agents.

Start with a Colorado license check

Every real estate broker who helps you buy or sell in Colorado must be licensed by the state. Titles on a website or business card are marketing; the license record is the fact. The Colorado Division of Real Estate publishes a public license lookup where you can search by name and confirm that the license is active. Pending, inactive or expired licensees may not perform actions that require a license.

Write down the broker’s full name and the brokerage they work under, then check both. If you are speaking with a team, ask which licensed broker will be named in your agreement and who else on the team will be involved. That short step prevents confusion later about who is responsible for what.

Ask each agent to explain Lakewood pricing

Lakewood includes detached homes, townhomes and condos, and areas such as Belmar and Green Mountain offer different settings. A city-wide figure says little about a specific address. When you interview a Lakewood real estate agent, ask how they would build a pricing or offer recommendation for one property: which nearby sales they would compare, how they adjust for condition and layout, and what they would want to see in person.

For sellers, a strong answer mentions recent comparable sales close to the home, differences in updates and lot characteristics, and how the county’s assessed value relates to, but is not the same as, market value. Our Lakewood home value guide explains those inputs in more detail. For buyers, a strong answer explains how the agent would evaluate a list price against recent sales and how they would handle a home that has been on the market longer than similar homes nearby.

Be cautious with any agent who quotes a precise value before seeing the property or reviewing sales. A thoughtful range with clear reasoning is more useful than a confident number with none.

Buyer’s agent vs. listing agent in Lakewood, CO

In Colorado, the working relationship is defined in writing. The state’s brokerage disclosure describes three main relationships. A seller’s agent works solely on behalf of the seller and advocates for the seller. A buyer’s agent works solely on behalf of the buyer and advocates for the buyer. A transaction-broker assists one or both parties through the transaction without being an agent or advocate for either, while still using reasonable skill and care. Read the Colorado Brokerage Disclosure to Buyer.

Colorado law also provides that a broker is considered a transaction-broker unless a single agency relationship is established through a written agreement. See the Colorado real estate license law. In practice, that means you should not assume advocacy; read which box is checked on the disclosure and ask the broker to explain it.

What to ask a Lakewood listing agent

Sellers comparing the best listing agent options in Lakewood, CO should ask for specifics they can compare side by side. Useful questions include:

  • Which recent nearby sales would you use to price my home, and why those?
  • What preparation would you recommend first, and what would you skip?
  • How will the home be photographed, described and shown, and who coordinates showings?
  • How will you present and compare offers, including terms beyond price?
  • What does the listing agreement say about duration, services and compensation?

Compensation for brokerage services is not set by law and is negotiable. Ask the agent to walk you through every term in the listing agreement before you sign. What to ask a Lakewood buyer’s agent

If you are buying, you will typically be asked to sign a written buyer agreement before touring homes with an agent. The National Association of REALTORS® explains that, as of August 17, 2024, agents who use an MLS and work with buyers must have a written agreement before touring a home, in person or by live virtual tour, and that the agreement must state compensation clearly and note that fees are fully negotiable. NAR consumer guide to written buyer agreements.

That makes the first conversation important. Ask a prospective buyer’s agent in Lakewood:

  • What services are included, and what is the length and scope of the agreement?
  • How will you help me compare homes across Lakewood and nearby cities?
  • How do you approach inspection findings and negotiation?
  • Who on your side will track contract deadlines?

Compare communication and availability

Real estate decisions often have short deadlines. Ask each agent how they prefer to communicate, how quickly you can expect a response during an active negotiation, and who covers when they are unavailable. Clarify who should be included in important decisions on your side, too, such as a co-buyer or co-owner.

Read reviews with a purpose

Reviews are most helpful when you read them for patterns rather than star counts alone. Look for comments about communication, preparation and how problems were handled. Read reviews on more than one platform, and notice whether they describe situations similar to yours, such as a first purchase, a sale with tight timing or a relocation.

Frequently asked questions

How many Lakewood real estate agents should I interview?+

There is no required number, but the Colorado Division of Real Estate recommends that buyers contact more than one broker and choose someone who fits their needs and knows the areas of interest. Speaking with two or three agents usually gives you enough contrast to compare pricing reasoning, communication style and the services described in the written agreement.

Is a Lakewood real estate agent different from a Denver real estate agent?+

A Colorado broker’s license is statewide, so the difference is local experience rather than legal authority. Because Lakewood is directly west of Denver, many agents work in both. Ask each agent how they would analyze sales and property types for the specific Lakewood or Denver addresses you are considering.

Do I have to sign an agreement before touring Lakewood homes?+

According to the National Association of REALTORS®, agents who use an MLS and work with buyers must enter a written agreement before touring a home, including live virtual tours. You do not need one to visit an open house on your own or to ask an agent about their services. Read the agreement’s services, duration and compensation terms before signing.

What is a transaction-broker in Colorado?+

A transaction-broker assists the buyer, the seller or both through a transaction without being an agent or advocate for either party, while using reasonable skill and care and disclosing known adverse material facts. Colorado law treats a broker as a transaction-broker unless a written single agency agreement is established.

Put your research to work

Bring the same questions to every agent you interview.

Write your questions down before the first call and ask each agent the same ones. Compare the reasoning behind each answer, not just the conclusion. A side-by-side page of notes makes it far easier to choose with confidence.

If you are not sure where to start, describe your timing, the Lakewood addresses or areas you are considering, and whether you are buying, selling or both. That is enough for a productive first conversation.

Make the first conversation count

Share your intended move, approximate timing and the main question you want answered. Avoid sending financial documents or account details through a general website form.
