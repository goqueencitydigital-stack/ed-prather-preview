Explore Colorado · A practical guide

Use the planner to explore community preferences, not to choose a home by score alone. Its percentage reflects selected preference tags only. Compare results with your budget, property needs, location-specific requirements, and due diligence, then download your brief to guide conversations and further research.

01

Read the result in context

Treat the result as a starting point for questions, not a recommendation about where to buy. The planner asks 12 questions, but only selected community-preference tags contribute to the community-fit percentage. Budget, bedroom count, timing, reasons for moving, and free-text responses do not change community order, so use those answers to shape your own search rather than read them into the score.

The percentage is the number of selected preference tags a community matches divided by the total number of selected preference tags, then rounded. When communities have the same percentage, they appear in alphabetical order. A high score therefore means a closer match to the tags you chose, not that a community is objectively better or more suitable in every respect.

02

Use an unscored result to explore

If you select no community preferences, the planner provides unscored exploration rather than a percentage-based comparison. That can still be useful: look through the communities presented, note what catches your attention, and identify which questions you would want answered before considering a move. You do not need to invent preferences just to make the tool produce a score.

You can download your own brief to keep your responses and notes together. Use it as a working document: add questions, flag priorities that are flexible, and record what you learn as you research. The brief is yours to use; it is not a property listing, a verification of community details, or a promise that a home matching your preferences is available.

03

Turn preferences into useful comparisons

Use the selected tags to make a short list of what you want to investigate, then compare communities on your own terms. For each one, write down what you would need to confirm, what could be a compromise, and what would make the location a poor fit for your daily routines. Separate firm requirements from preferences so that a close percentage does not obscure a non-negotiable need.

Budget and bedroom answers may help you clarify your own search, but they do not affect the planner’s community percentages. The planner compares preference tags; it does not match listings through the MLS, assess affordability, or compare commute times. It also does not match you with residents, rank schools or safety, or show listing availability. Relocation planning is an in-browser checklist. If you move on to the COHouseHunters search, treat it as a separate browsing step; the planner score does not validate or guarantee any listing.

04

Check the commitments behind an HOA

If a community includes a homeowners association, look beyond the fit score and ask what membership would involve. Colorado’s Division of Real Estate recommends reviewing the association documents available under section 7 of the Colorado Contract to Buy and Sell (Residential); those documents include governing and financial information that can help you understand how the HOA operates. Colorado Division of Real Estate: Buying a Home in a HOA Ask your broker to help obtain the documents from the seller. Colorado Division of Real Estate: Buying a Home in a HOA

Review regular dues, recent meeting minutes, the budget, and any discussion of special assessments or upcoming costs. The Division advises buyers to consider costs beyond regular dues and notes that an HOA’s executive board makes most financial decisions. Colorado Division of Real Estate: Buying a Home in a HOA Colorado law provides some standards for HOA finances, but the Division says there is no regulatory oversight of HOAs. Colorado Division of Real Estate: Buying a Home in a HOA

05

Look closely at maintenance and insurance

Ask what the association maintains and what remains the homeowner’s responsibility. Review the governing documents for the definition of common elements. Colorado Division of Real Estate: Buying a Home in a HOA The Division explains that state law gives limited direction on HOA maintenance and landscaping, and that the executive board determines how and when work is performed. Colorado Division of Real Estate: Buying a Home in a HOA Also look at the community’s current maintenance and landscaping.

Ask for the association’s insurance information and find out whether the HOA requires homeowners to carry additional coverage. Colorado’s Common Interest Ownership Act requires associations to maintain specified property insurance on common elements and commercial general liability coverage. Colorado Division of Real Estate: Buying a Home in a HOA Attached-home buyers may want to understand how the association’s coverage and their own policy work together. Discuss your coverage questions with an insurance professional.

06

Keep property checks separate from preferences

A community preference is not a substitute for investigating a particular home. Build a separate checklist for the property: arrange appropriate inspections, review disclosures and documents, and ask qualified professionals about issues that matter to the home and its location. For an HOA property, include the association’s governing and financial records as well as property-specific questions; the planner does not assess either set of materials.

Radon is one topic to handle through transaction-specific information and appropriate testing rather than a community score. Colorado’s public health department provides a required radon brochure for Colorado real estate transactions and rental agreements, along with resources about measuring and mitigating radon. Colorado Department of Public Health and Environment: Radon and real estate Read the applicable materials and ask a qualified radon professional about testing or mitigation questions.

07

Verify local details before deciding

Use your brief to guide focused research: identify the location, property type, and questions you still need answered, then seek information from relevant sources and professionals. If you consult municipal guidance written for Denver, treat it as Denver-specific rather than a statewide rule. For a home elsewhere, check with the appropriate local authority about requirements that apply to that address.

A practical next step is to revisit your shortlist after checking the association documents, property condition, insurance responsibilities, and location-specific requirements. If new information changes what matters to you, update your notes and consider whether your selected tags still reflect your priorities. The planner can help organize preferences, but the final comparison should include evidence about the actual home and community.

Before your next move

Questions & answers.

What does the community-fit percentage measure?+

It measures the share of your selected community-preference tags that each community matches, with the result rounded. It does not incorporate your budget, bedroom count, timeline, reasons for moving, or free-text responses. Use the percentage as a narrow preference comparison, then investigate the details that matter to your decision.

Why are two communities shown with the same percentage?+

They may match the same number of the selected preference tags. When percentages tie, the planner orders those communities alphabetically. That ordering is not an additional rating or a claim that one community is a stronger match; compare their details and decide what questions to research next.

Can the planner tell me whether a home is affordable or available?+

No. Budget answers do not rank communities, and the planner does not verify affordability or show listing availability. Use a separate property search for current listings and assess costs for the specific home, including any association dues or potential assessments. Consult appropriate professionals for personal financial questions.

What should I review before buying in an HOA?+

Request and review the association’s governing and financial documents, including its budget and recent meeting minutes. Look into dues, possible special assessments, maintenance responsibilities, and insurance. Colorado’s Division of Real Estate recommends reviewing the documents available under section 7 of the residential purchase contract; your broker can assist with obtaining them.
