01

Turn your move into a usable planning brief

Start with the in-browser planning checklist to organize three things: when you hope to move, which destinations you are considering, and what matters most to you. Add practical priorities such as commute needs, access to services, preferred home features, and flexibility on timing. Separate firm requirements from preferences so that a promising property does not quietly change the plan you meant to follow.

When your entries reflect your current thinking, download your own generated planning brief and keep it with your relocation notes. The checklist organizes information; it does not book an agent, arrange movers or contractors, confirm appointments, or send automated notifications. If a deadline or visit matters, record it in your own calendar and contact the relevant person or service directly.

02

Prepare before you scout

Before traveling, write down what you need to learn at each destination and what evidence would help you compare it. List the locations you want to visit, the questions you need answered, and the people you need to contact. Group visits by area and allow time for travel, meals, and note-taking. A plan with fewer, purposeful stops can be easier to evaluate than a packed itinerary.

Make a property question sheet before requesting tours. Include your intended use, desired move-in window, must-have features, and unresolved concerns. Ask who can provide the relevant records, when they may be available, and whether a follow-up visit or specialist review could affect your decision timeline. Keep confirmed arrangements separate from ideas; the checklist does not confirm tours or contact providers on your behalf.

03

Compare destinations using your own priorities

Choose a short set of criteria that reflects your household’s needs, then use the same criteria for each destination. You might compare travel time to places you regularly go, access to the services you use, the setting you prefer, and how well the area fits your planned routine. Record observations and questions separately so that a first impression is not mistaken for a verified detail.

Use external COHouseHunters search separately from the planning checklist. Preference-tag matching in the checklist is not MLS search, affordability screening, or commute matching. The checklist is not a booking tool or an automatic source of updates to your downloaded brief. Review search results in that service, note the property details you want to check, and verify important information with the appropriate source. Do not assume a saved planning brief confirms a listing, an appointment, or a property’s current status.

04

Schedule scouting around dependencies

Work backward from your intended decision date. First identify which questions require an in-person visit, which records you can request in advance, and which conversations depend on hearing back from someone else. Then set a realistic order for calls, visits, and follow-ups. Keep a short list of open items so you can tell whether a delay is a scheduling issue or missing information.

For each visit, save the address, contact details, agreed time, and purpose in your own calendar or notes. Build in a backup plan for a canceled visit and leave space to revisit a property or destination after reviewing records. If a professional’s input is important to your decision, contact an appropriately qualified person directly and ask about their availability and scope.

05

Ask for property records and distinguish preferences from requirements

If a property is in a homeowners association, request and review the governing and financial documents available through the purchase process. The Colorado Division of Real Estate says buyers under contract are entitled to the association documentation listed in section 7 of the Colorado residential purchase contract, and suggests asking a broker to help obtain it. Colorado Division of Real Estate: Buying a Home in a HOA

Review budgets, meeting minutes, regular dues, possible special assessments, maintenance responsibilities, and insurance questions; these are different from your preferences for a home’s finishes or layout. The state advises buyers to assess an HOA’s financial condition, potential costs, insurance, and maintenance practices before purchasing. Colorado Division of Real Estate: Buying a Home in a HOA For inspections, required disclosures, and other transaction documents, ask your broker or the relevant professional what applies to your purchase.

06

Include site conditions and safety questions

For a property near wildland vegetation, make time to observe the structure and its surroundings, then ask what mitigation work has been done and what upkeep may be needed. The Colorado State Forest Service recommends addressing the home ignition zone, including the structure and surrounding area, and describes defensible-space zones extending outward from buildings. Colorado State Forest Service: Protect Your Home & Property from Wildfire

Treat visible features as prompts for questions, not as a substitute for an inspection or professional assessment. Ask which areas are maintained by the owner or an association, whether a qualified professional should evaluate a concern, and what records are available. Colorado’s health department provides information about radon in real estate transactions and links to a required brochure for Colorado real estate transactions and rental agreements. Colorado Department of Public Health and Environment: Radon and real estate

07

After scouting, turn open questions into next steps

After each visit, update your own notes while observations are fresh. Mark each item as verified, still unknown, or a preference; write down who can answer each open question and when you plan to follow up. Compare destinations and properties against the same priorities you used before scouting. If a new concern changes your priorities, revise your plan before committing to a new schedule.

Add official registration tasks to your timeline once you know where and when you will establish residence. For people who establish Colorado residency and are subject to the standard new-resident requirements, the Colorado DMV says to transfer a driver’s license within 30 days and title and register a vehicle within 90 days after establishing residency; its page describes residency criteria and the relevant processes. Colorado DMV: Welcome to Colorado Check the rules and services for the specific county or municipality where you will live rather than assuming every local process is statewide.

Questions & answers.

What does the relocation checklist do?+

It helps you organize move timing, destination options, and personal priorities in one planning process. You can download your own generated planning brief for reference. It does not book an agent, arrange moving or contractor services, confirm appointments, or send automated notifications, so handle those arrangements directly with the people or services involved.

Can the planning brief confirm a property or appointment?+

No. The brief is a planning aid, not confirmation of a listing, property detail, tour, or appointment. Use the external COHouseHunters search separately, check important property information with the relevant source, and contact the person or service directly to arrange or confirm a visit.

What should I request when considering a home in an HOA?+

Ask for the association’s governing and financial documents, then review costs, meeting minutes, maintenance responsibilities, and insurance questions. For Colorado buyers under contract, section 7 of the residential purchase contract identifies association documents buyers are entitled to receive; ask your broker for help obtaining them. Colorado Division of Real Estate: Buying a Home in a HOA

When should I handle Colorado driver and vehicle registration tasks?+

Add these tasks to your move timeline and verify which process applies to you through the Colorado DMV. For people who establish Colorado residency and are subject to the standard new-resident requirements, the DMV says to transfer a driver’s license within 30 days and title and register a vehicle within 90 days after establishing residency. Colorado DMV: Welcome to Colorado
