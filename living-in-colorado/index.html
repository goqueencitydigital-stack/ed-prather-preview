Make room for the Colorado life you want.

Start with the places and routines that matter to you.

Explore Colorado · A practical guide

A successful Colorado move starts with the life you will live most days, not only the activities that drew you to visit. Compare routines and routes, ask property-specific questions across seasons, review ownership records, prepare for outdoor conditions, and work through address-level checks before committing.

Updated November 6, 2026

01

Plan for everyday life, not just a visit

Treat relocation as a shift in ordinary routines, not simply a search for weekend scenery. Before choosing an area, write down what a typical Tuesday requires: work start, school or care runs if relevant, grocery and pharmacy stops, exercise, appointments, and time at home. Then compare that list with what you actually do now, not only with the activities you hope to do on vacation.

Make a short “must work” list and a separate “would be nice” list. Include practical details such as storage, workspace, guest visits, pets, and how often you expect to leave home. For each candidate address, ask what would change in your day and what you would need to give up. That comparison can help distinguish an appealing trip from a workable long-term routine.

02

Test transport and everyday access

Map the trips you make most often from each address, then test the routes at the times you would actually travel. A map estimate is a starting point, not a substitute for experiencing a route. If transit matters, check the stops, service times, transfers, and the walk from the home. If you drive, try the full trip, including the approach to parking or the final walk.

Repeat the test under a different schedule or condition where possible, and keep notes rather than relying on a single impression. Consider backup routes and how you would handle errands if one household vehicle were unavailable. Ask the seller or property manager about access arrangements that matter to you, but verify the details yourself with the relevant transit or road information.

03

Ask seasonal questions about the property

A showing captures one moment. Ask how the home and its surroundings function through the rest of the year: where water goes during heavy rain or snowmelt, which areas become icy, how outdoor spaces are maintained, and what seasonal tasks fall to the owner. Look for records, receipts, or maintenance notes that help explain the answers, and note anything you want a qualified inspector to examine.

Ask what systems or exterior features need recurring attention, who is responsible for each task, and whether the seller knows of past repairs or recurring problems. Compare the answers with the property’s visible condition and the documents available. A preference, such as a particular view or outdoor feature, is not a substitute for inspections; make separate plans for evaluating the home’s condition and the features you simply want.

04

Review ownership and community records

If the home is in a homeowners association, request the association records available through the purchase process and review both its governing and financial documents. Colorado’s Division of Real Estate says buyers under the residential contract are entitled to the documents listed in Section 7, and recommends asking a broker for help obtaining them. Colorado Division of Real Estate: Buying a Home in a HOA

Read the rules for restrictions that could affect your plans, and check meeting minutes for discussion of possible assessments or unresolved maintenance matters. The state guidance also recommends determining whether the association uses a community association manager and researching the manager or company. Colorado Division of Real Estate: Buying a Home in a HOA Write down unclear terms and ask the appropriate professional to explain them before you rely on an assumption.

05

Compare costs, coverage, and responsibilities

For an HOA home, compare regular dues with possible additional costs rather than treating the monthly assessment as the whole picture. The Colorado Division of Real Estate advises buyers to consider special assessments and other unanticipated costs, and to review recent meeting minutes for assessments under consideration. Colorado Division of Real Estate: Buying a Home in a HOA Ask what each recurring charge covers and which repairs or services remain the owner’s responsibility.

Clarify insurance boundaries before closing: what the association insures, what you must insure, and whether the community has requirements for additional coverage. Colorado’s HOA guidance describes required coverage for common elements, while emphasizing that buyers should determine what insurance the association maintains and whether owners need additional protection. Colorado Division of Real Estate: Buying a Home in a HOA For questions about policy terms or coverage, consult an insurance professional.

06

Prepare for outdoor conditions at the address

If the address is near wildland vegetation, ask what work has been done to reduce fire exposure and what ongoing tasks are expected. The Colorado State Forest Service recommends reducing fuels around structures and maintaining defensible space; its guide divides that space into zones extending outward from the home. Colorado State Forest Service: Protect Your Home & Property from Wildfire Check which actions are practical on the lot and whether the HOA or another party controls the relevant area.

Include radon in your property questions. Colorado provides a radon brochure for real estate transactions and rental agreements, and state resources cover measurement and mitigation. Colorado Department of Public Health and Environment: Radon and real estate Ask what testing information is available, whether a test is part of your planned inspections, and who can explain the results or mitigation options. Keep this separate from preference-based checks such as views, finishes, or yard layout.

07

Organize moving tasks and local checks

Build a moving checklist around the actual address and your household’s needs. Identify utility providers, internet options, mail forwarding, medical-record transfers, and any appointments or registrations that need lead time. Confirm service details with providers rather than assuming a neighborhood name guarantees coverage. Keep copies of purchase, inspection, insurance, and association records together so you can find them when arranging services or following up on a property question.

Colorado’s motor vehicle guidance says new residents must transfer a driver’s license within 30 days and title and register a vehicle within 90 days after establishing residency; check the state’s current instructions for your circumstances. Colorado Department of Revenue, Motor Vehicle: Welcome to Colorado For local requirements, verify directly with the city or county serving the address. Do not assume a Denver-specific rule applies elsewhere in Colorado, or that a rule elsewhere applies in Denver.

Before your next move

Questions & answers.

What should I compare before choosing a Colorado home?+

Compare the home against your ordinary routines: commute or transit, errands, work needs, storage, and outdoor responsibilities. Visit at the times you expect to travel, then make a separate list of property features you prefer. Keep preference checks distinct from inspections and document review, which address condition, responsibilities, and records rather than whether a home suits your style.

Which HOA records should I ask to review?+

For a Colorado HOA purchase, review the governing and financial documents listed in Section 7 of the residential contract, and ask your broker for help obtaining them. Pay attention to rules, budgets, meeting minutes, maintenance responsibilities, insurance, and possible assessments. Colorado Division of Real Estate: Buying a Home in a HOA

Should I ask about radon before buying?+

Yes. Ask what radon information or test results are available and discuss whether testing should be included in your inspection plan. Colorado’s public health department provides a required radon brochure for real estate transactions and resources on measuring and mitigating radon. Colorado Department of Public Health and Environment: Radon and real estate A qualified professional can help interpret results.

What should I check if a home is near wildland vegetation?+

Ask what wildfire mitigation work has been completed, who maintains the surrounding space, and which tasks you would inherit. The Colorado State Forest Service recommends reducing nearby fuel and maintaining defensible space around structures. Colorado State Forest Service: Protect Your Home & Property from Wildfire A local fire department or trained natural-resource professional can help with address-specific questions.
