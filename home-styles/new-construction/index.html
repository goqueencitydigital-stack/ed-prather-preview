Explore New Construction homes.

Start with the details that matter to your ownership experience, then compare real properties with your agent.

Explore Colorado · A practical guide

A model home can help you picture a new build, but your decisions should rest on the written specifications, lot details, costs, inspection access, warranty terms and schedule. Use this guide to compare options, request useful records, and identify which questions need answers before you commit.

Updated November 9, 2026

01

Separate the model home from the written specifications

Treat a model home as a demonstration, not a complete description of the home you are buying. Make a side-by-side list of what you see on site, what the builder says is included, and what the written purchase documents specify. Ask the builder to identify any displayed features or finishes that are optional, upgraded, or simply not part of your selected plan.

Before signing, request the current written specifications for your exact home and make sure they identify the selected plan, included features, and chosen options clearly enough for you to compare them with the model. If a sales conversation changes your understanding, ask for the documents to be updated. Keep copies of the signed documents and any written selections together.

02

Compare the actual lot and phase

Compare specific lots rather than choosing from a plan name or a model-home visit alone. Ask for the lot number, a site or plot plan, the home’s proposed placement, and any written information about the lot that the builder provides. Note what matters to you, such as orientation, nearby construction, access, privacy, or outdoor space, and ask what can be confirmed in writing.

If you are considering homes in different phases, request the current phase map and ask what is planned around each lot, what remains undecided, and when the builder expects to update that information. Do not assume a rendering or verbal description is a final commitment. Record unanswered questions and follow up with the builder before selecting a lot or agreeing to a change.

03

Make allowances and changes easy to track

Ask for a written list of allowances, including what each allowance covers, its stated amount, and how selections or costs above that amount will be handled. Confirm selection deadlines and whether the allowance includes related work, materials, delivery, or installation. If a document uses a term you do not understand, ask the builder to explain it in writing before you rely on it.

For each requested change, keep a record of the description, price adjustment, any effect on the schedule, and the required approval process. Ask whether work can proceed before the change is fully documented, and avoid relying on a text or conversation as your only record. Maintain one current list of approved changes and compare it with the contract documents before closing.

04

Get a schedule you can monitor

Ask the builder for the current construction schedule and the milestones used to track progress. Find out how you will receive updates, who will provide them, and how changes to projected dates are communicated. Request written clarification about dates that matter to your plans, such as selection deadlines, walk-throughs, and the anticipated completion or closing window.

Use the schedule as a planning document, not as a promise unless the written agreement says otherwise. Ask what could change the dates and how the builder will notify you if a milestone moves. Keep dated copies of schedule updates and compare them with any commitments in your purchase documents. If timing is important to your move, discuss your contingencies with your real estate professional.

05

Clarify inspection access and responsibilities

Ask the builder, before you commit, whether an independent home inspector may visit during construction and at which stages. Confirm access rules, scheduling lead time, site-safety requirements, and how inspection findings should be submitted. If access is limited, request the policy in writing and ask what other opportunities you will have to review the home before closing.

Keep inspections distinct from finish preferences: a preferred selection is not an inspection, and an inspection does not replace your review of the written specifications. Ask a qualified home inspector what a proposed inspection can cover and when it is useful. Also confirm which inspections or approvals the builder says will occur, who performs them, and what records, if any, you will receive.

06

Read the warranty scope before relying on it

Request the warranty documents before closing, not just a brochure or summary. Check the written scope, start date, duration, exclusions, maintenance responsibilities, claim instructions, and any deadlines for reporting a concern. Ask who administers the warranty and whether the builder, another company, or more than one party handles different types of claims.

Ask how to submit a claim, what supporting records to keep, and what response process the documents describe. Compare any verbal explanation with the written warranty and request written clarification where they differ or leave a question open. Keep the final warranty, home-care guidance, inspection records, and builder contact details together so you can find them when needed.

07

Review HOA documents and local requirements

If the home is in a homeowners association, request the association’s governing and financial documents and review them before closing. Colorado’s Division of Real Estate explains that buyers under the Colorado Contract to Buy and Sell (Residential) are entitled to the association documents listed in section 7, and recommends reviewing them to understand the HOA’s operations and financial condition. Colorado Division of Real Estate: Buying a Home in a HOA

Ask about regular dues, possible special assessments, maintenance responsibilities, insurance, and any design or use restrictions that could affect your plans; review the records rather than relying only on a sales summary. Denver’s zoning page explains that zoning regulates property use and development in Denver, including matters such as structure location and allowed uses. City and County of Denver: Zoning Permits That is Denver-specific guidance, so visitors elsewhere should verify requirements with their local jurisdiction. Colorado’s public health department lists a radon brochure for real estate transactions. Colorado Department of Public Health and Environment: Radon and real estate Ask your real estate professional how it applies to your transaction.

Questions & answers.

Can I rely on the model home to show what I will receive?+

Use the model to understand the builder’s presentation, then verify the home you are buying through its written specifications and signed selections. Ask the builder to identify any displayed features that are optional or excluded. If the documents do not clearly answer a question, request clarification in writing before you commit.

What should I ask about allowances?+

Request a written breakdown of each allowance, what it covers, selection deadlines, and how amounts above the allowance are handled. Ask whether related materials or work are included. Keep selections and any price or schedule changes documented, and make sure the final record matches what you approved.

Can I hire an independent inspector during construction?+

Ask the builder about inspection access, timing, scheduling, and site rules before signing. Confirm the policy in writing and discuss suitable inspection stages with a qualified home inspector. Do not assume access or that a preferred finish selection counts as an inspection; clarify what inspections and records the builder says are included.

Which HOA records should I review before buying?+

Request the governing and financial documents listed in section 7 of the Colorado residential purchase contract and review them for operating information, costs, maintenance, insurance, and possible special assessments. Colorado Division of Real Estate: Buying a Home in a HOA Ask your broker or real estate professional to help obtain the records and identify questions for the association.
