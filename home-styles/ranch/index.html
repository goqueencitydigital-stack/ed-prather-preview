Explore ranch homes.

Start with the details that matter to your ownership experience, then compare real properties with your agent.

Explore Colorado · A practical guide

When considering a ranch home, compare the entry, garage, laundry, basement and main-floor layout. Investigate renovation options, roof condition and yard work, and verify whether each property fits your needs before you buy.

Updated November 9, 2026

01

Start with the daily routine

Colorado’s historic architecture guide describes the ranch as a one-story building type with a low, horizontal profile; it also notes that many examples have an attached garage and a rear porch or patio. Colorado’s postwar residential architecture guide Those features can make a ranch worth considering if you want everyday rooms on one level, but the label alone does not tell you how the home works for you.

Picture an ordinary day, not just a showing: where do groceries come in, where do coats and shoes go, and how far is the bedroom from the bathroom? Try the route with the household members, mobility aids or carried items that matter to you. Note thresholds, narrow turns, door clearances and changes in floor height, then compare those observations across homes.

02

Check every route in and out

A ranch can still involve stairs at the front entry, between a garage and the living area, or down to a basement laundry room. During a tour, walk each route you would use in a typical week. Count steps, check handrails and lighting, and notice whether a door swing, landing or tight turn makes carrying items awkward.

Ask whether the garage entry is level with the main floor, whether there is another usable entrance, and where laundry is located. If any step is a concern, do not assume a ramp or lift can be added easily. Ask a qualified contractor or accessibility professional to assess the actual site, clearances and possible changes before treating a feature as a workable solution.

03

Compare basement and main-floor space

A finished basement may add useful rooms, but consider whether you would need those rooms every day or mainly for guests, storage or hobbies. If laundry, a bathroom or a bedroom is downstairs, decide whether that arrangement fits your needs now and over time. On the main floor, check that the rooms you depend on are connected in a practical way.

For a basement, ask what work has been done and request available permits, receipts, warranties and contractor details. Ask for any radon test results and mitigation records; Colorado’s health department identifies a radon brochure as required for real estate transactions and rental agreements. Colorado Department of Public Health and Environment: Radon and real estate Treat the brochure and seller records as documents to review, not as a substitute for evaluating the specific home.

04

Test renovation ideas before relying on them

If the layout almost works, write down the change you have in mind: relocating laundry, opening a doorway, combining rooms or creating a main-floor sleeping area. Check whether the desired space already has the needed plumbing, electrical service, ventilation and access. A floorplan sketch can help clarify the idea, but it cannot confirm what is feasible inside the walls.

Ask the seller what alterations have been made and request supporting permits, plans, invoices and inspection records where available. For changes you are considering, get an on-site opinion from an appropriately qualified contractor or design professional before making the home contingent on that plan. Separate “I prefer this layout” from “this work is possible, approved and safe”; each needs different verification.

05

Look beyond the interior to roof and yard work

The Colorado architecture guide lists low-pitched roofs and wide overhanging eaves among common ranch elements, and notes that an attached garage can extend the home’s profile. Colorado’s postwar residential architecture guide For a particular property, inspect the roof rather than assuming its age or condition from the home’s style. Request available installation, repair and warranty records.

Walk the full yard and note the work it may require: mowing, pruning, leaf cleanup, snow clearing and care for patios or paths. Look for places where water collects or where access to outdoor areas is uneven. Ask who currently handles seasonal tasks and what equipment or maintenance records will remain, then decide whether the space is manageable for your routine.

06

Compare homes and confirm the details

Use the external COHouseHunters search, if helpful, as a discovery tool rather than proof that a home meets your needs. Preference-tag matching is not MLS, affordability or commute matching. The relocation guide is an in-browser checklist. Compare candidate homes with the same checklist: entry steps, garage route, laundry location, main-floor essentials, basement use, roof documentation and yard workload. Confirm listing details with the listing contact and review property-specific records before relying on a description or photograph.

If the property is in Denver, the city says zoning regulates matters including structure size and location, allowed uses, parking and signs. City and County of Denver: Zoning Permits That page describes Denver, not rules for all of Colorado. For a planned addition or change of use elsewhere, contact the relevant local jurisdiction to confirm applicable requirements.

Before your next move

Questions & answers.

Does a ranch home guarantee stair-free living?+

No. A ranch is generally described as a one-story building type, but a specific home may have steps at an entrance, between the garage and main floor, or down to a basement. Walk every route you would use and verify thresholds, clearances and handrails in person. For an accessibility question, seek a property-specific professional assessment.

Should I count a basement as everyday living space?+

Count it only if its access and layout work for the activities you expect to use it for. Check whether laundry, a bathroom or a room you rely on is downstairs, and evaluate the stairs and lighting. Request available renovation records and radon documents, then arrange appropriate property-specific evaluations rather than relying on a listing description.

What records should I request about renovations?+

Ask for available permits, plans, contractor invoices, inspection records, warranties and receipts for major work. Match the records to the changes you can see, and ask follow-up questions about work without documentation. If a planned alteration is important to your decision, have a qualified professional inspect the home and assess feasibility before you depend on it.

How can I compare roof and yard maintenance?+

Ask for available roof installation, repair and warranty records, then arrange an appropriate inspection of the specific roof. Walk the yard and list recurring tasks such as mowing, pruning, cleanup and path maintenance. Compare the work against your time, abilities and preferences rather than assuming a smaller-looking or single-level home will require less upkeep.
