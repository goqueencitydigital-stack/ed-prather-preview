Put your research to work

Make a home-tour request useful for everyone involved.

Provide the property addresses or listing references you want to discuss, the time windows that could work and any access needs the team should know about. If you are already represented by another real estate professional, include that information in the conversation.

A requested showing is not a confirmed appointment. Availability and access need to be coordinated before you travel to the property. Wait for confirmation of the address, meeting arrangements and time, and let the team know if your plans change.

Choose what you need to learn at each home

Bring a short list of layout and everyday-use questions. Look at storage, entry routes, room relationships and the areas you expect to use most. Record condition questions for appropriate professional follow-up rather than treating a showing as an inspection.

Compare observations while they are fresh

After each visit, note what worked, what did not and what would change your decision. Keep unknowns visible. Use the comparison tool to organize your own notes without assuming it retrieves or verifies live listing details.

Good to know

Frequently asked questions

Is my showing confirmed when I send the request?

No. The team contacts you to coordinate a time. Showings depend on seller and listing availability, so a time is only confirmed once the team has arranged it with you.

What should I include in my request?

Add the addresses or listing links you want to see, the days and times that work for you, who will attend and any questions you already have about each home.

Can I ask to see more than one home?

Yes. List every home you are interested in and the team can help plan the visits.
